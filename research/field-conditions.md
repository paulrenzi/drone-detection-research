# Front-line conditions in Ukraine
{: .no_toc }

What the ground actually looks like for Ukrainian infantry in 2024–2026, and what that means for the sensor models on this site. Every point is sourced. Soldiers' and volunteers' accounts are marked as **accounts**; they describe one place and time, not the whole front. Where nothing was found, this page says so instead of guessing.

1. TOC
{:toc}

## Where infantry actually sit: tree lines

- **The windbreak tree line is the position.** Eastern Ukraine is open farmland cut by planted windbreaks. Open steppe gives almost no cover from drones, so the tree lines serve as cover, gathering points and covered footpaths to the front. A National Guard gunner: "The tree line is life." Russian troops use them the same way ([Stars and Stripes / Washington Post, Oct 2024](https://www.stripes.com/theaters/europe/2024-10-28/ukraine-farming-vital-battlefield-land-15653560.html)).
- **The belts are narrow.** One belt in Luhansk is described as a few yards wide (same source). Ukrainian agroforestry guidance plans belts of 3–5 rows, about 25–49 ft wide ([IUAF](https://iuaf.org/ukraine/amp/)); that is the planting design, not what is left of them after shelling. Some front-line belts have been reduced to "piles of split wood" ([CEPA, 2023](https://cepa.org/article/ukraines-war-of-the-treelines/)).
- **Positions are small and hidden.** Account: near Pokrovsk in January 2025, an open trench was destroyed and eight men put out of action in under a day, while a concealed position of two fighting holes with three riflemen each held for ten days, linked by two radios and resupplied by drone ([War on the Rocks](https://warontherocks.com/the-strange-transformation-of-the-front-line-in-ukraine/)).
- **Elsewhere it's ruins.** On parts of the southern front the route to positions runs through damaged streets, with "no fields or forest strips" ([Texty](https://texty.org.ua/articles/117451/why-ukrainians-soldiers-havent-left-their-combat-positions-for-months/)).
- **Not found:** any figure for how much of the fighting happens in tree lines versus open ground versus towns, or how far apart tree lines are on the actual front.
- **Not found:** any published figure, in English or Ukrainian, for how far positions sit from a tree-line edge. What is published:
  - Belts are planned at 3–5 rows, about 25–49 ft wide ([IUAF](https://iuaf.org/ukraine/amp/)); one Luhansk belt is a few yards wide ([Stars and Stripes](https://www.stripes.com/theaters/europe/2024-10-28/ukraine-farming-vital-battlefield-land-15653560.html)).
  - Infantry live in hand-dug burrows about a yard wide, with underground rooms and tunnels, in tree belts, river floodplains, steppe and slag heaps. The source gives no location relative to a tree line, and says Russian forces control the sky over forest belts ([Texty](https://texty.org.ua/articles/118179/chym-dovshe-tym-krashche-yak-pixota-vyzhyvaye-v-norax/)).
  - A belt believed occupied may be empty but under constant enemy drone watch ([450 Tactical](https://450tactical.com/yak-diiaty-pid-chas-shturmu-posadky-korotko-zhorstko-pravylno/)).
  - Some fighting is in real forest, on foot, such as the Serebryansky forest near Kreminna ([Kyiv Post](https://www.kyivpost.com/post/52057); [CEPA](https://cepa.org/article/firefights-and-fear-in-ukraines-forest-war/)).
  - **Inference, not sourced:** in a 25–49 ft windbreak no point is more than about 13–26 ft from an edge, so a wire to the edge is yards to tens of yards. In real forest it could be hundreds of yards. In ruins or open ground there is no tree edge, so a receiver would go on a roof or pole near the position. Because belts are watched, some units may dig beside belts rather than in them; no source says how often. Test T18 [asks]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link research/problem-check.md %}#the-next-step-ask-before-building-t18).

## Seasons

- **Leaf fall strips cover.** Autumn thins the tree lines and forces units to move ([Stars and Stripes](https://www.stripes.com/theaters/europe/2024-10-28/ukraine-farming-vital-battlefield-land-15653560.html); [CEPA](https://cepa.org/article/ukraines-war-of-the-treelines/)). A Ukrainian drone operator says spring foliage cuts the effectiveness of observation quadcopters every year, as infantry move through green tree lines ([Euromaidan Press, Mar 2026](https://euromaidanpress.com/2026/03/05/a-ukrainian-drone-operator-explains-russias-spring-advantage-and-the-two-things-that-can-neutralize-it/)).
- **Winter:** snow hides mines and tripwires, soldiers are exposed crossing open fields, and frozen ground is hard to dig ([Lviv Herald](https://www.lvivherald.com/post/ukrainian-troops-in-winter-2025); [Euromaidan Press, Feb 2026](https://euromaidanpress.com/2026/02/19/its-really-tough-winter-no-cover-drones-can-see-people-walking-canadian-volunteer-describes-harsh-realities-on-ukraines-front-lines/)).
- **Cold hurts drones and batteries.** A Ukrainian brigade reports FPVs becoming less reliable in cold weather ([ArmyInform, Jan 2026](https://armyinform.com.ua/en/2026/01/30/in-cold-weather-fpvs-are-more-capricious-bomb-droppers-more-stable-rubizh-brigade-explains-how-cold-affects-drones)). A Swedish analysis notes hobby-grade platforms are typically rated to about 14 °F against about −22 °F for military systems ([KKrVA](https://kkrva.se/winter-is-coming-what-to-think-about-conducting-drone-operations-in-cold-weather/)).
- **Not found:** measured battery or flight-time loss at −4 °F, front-line reporting on spring and autumn mud, or wind data.

## The drone kill zone

- **It is miles deep, and growing.** Estimates vary by source and sector:
  - A sergeant of the 24th Brigade: the grey zone grew from 0.31–1.2 mi to about 3.1–4.3 mi ([Militarnyi](https://militarnyi.com/en/articles/the-kill-zone-of-modern-warfare-size-and-structure-control-and-means-of-destruction-survival-and-shifting-the-lines/)).
  - A National Guard spokesman: the most intense zone is 3.1–6.2 mi, with strikes reaching about 19 mi ([Euromaidan Press, Dec 2025](https://euromaidanpress.com/2025/12/24/kill-zones-everywhere-ukrainian-military-describes-new-combination-replacing-mass-assaults-across-main-fronts/)).
  - A ground-robot commander: even 9 miles out is a zone of extreme danger ([LRT](https://www.lrt.lt/en/news-in-english/19/2869171/inside-ukraine-s-kill-zone-you-can-be-killed-very-quickly-and-out-of-nowhere)).
- **Infantry walk in, at night.** Account: after leaving vehicles, infantry walk 2 to 4 miles, and often 5 to 9, to positions; vehicles that drive up are destroyed ([Texty](https://texty.org.ua/articles/117451/why-ukrainians-soldiers-havent-left-their-combat-positions-for-months/)).
- **They stay a long time.** Units have held positions for 90, 200 and sometimes 240–280 days (same source). A newer order caps infantry time on the front line at two months, with up to a month more to complete a rotation ([Kyiv Post](https://www.kyivpost.com/amp/post/75270)).
- **Resupply comes by drone and ground robot**, including heavy bomber drones delivering to positions ([Texty](https://texty.org.ua/articles/117451/why-ukrainians-soldiers-havent-left-their-combat-positions-for-months/)).
- **Fibre-optic FPVs reach 6.2–12 mi.** A Ukrainian drone-team commander: most spools are 6.2 mi, some 9.3 mi, and 12 mi spools are in testing. They help most in forests and among buildings ([Kyiv Independent, May 2025](https://kyivindependent.com/as-russias-fiber-optic-drones-flood-the-battlefield-ukraine-is-racing-to-catch-up/)).
- **Some wait in ambush.** Analysts quoted by Trench Art say Russian fibre-optic FPVs land on or beside roads and wait, up to 12 hours by one estimate, then strike passing vehicles; one unit saturates 330–980 ft stretches of road ([Trench Art](https://www.trenchart.us/p/russias-best-drones-lie-in-wait-beside)). A drone sitting silent on the ground makes no sound until it takes off.

## Electronic warfare

- **Satellite navigation is jammed.** Russia's R-330Zh Zhitel jams GPS and mobile-phone signals, with a stated range of 12–16 mi against ground radios and at least 31 mi against aircraft ([Kyiv Post](https://www.kyivpost.com/analysis/32435)). Ukrainian strike drones have moved to visual navigation for the final approach because of jamming ([IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/ukraine-killer-drones)). **Not found:** a measured share of the front where GNSS is unusable.
- **Emitters are hunted.** Russian radio reconnaissance locates enemy radio signals, and the Leer-3 system poses as a phone mast to find clusters of phones and the units carrying them ([Kyiv Post](https://www.kyivpost.com/analysis/32435)). Units avoid generators partly because they "can be heard for miles" ([Texty](https://texty.org.ua/articles/117451/why-ukrainians-soldiers-havent-left-their-combat-positions-for-months/)).
- **Drone jamming is dense along the whole front.** RUSI counted at least one major Russian jamming system per 6 mi (10 km) of front ([via Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_Warfare_Troops_of_the_Russian_Federation); a 2023 figure). Since late 2024 Russian anti-drone jammers have stood on masts, trees and buildings 0.6–9.3 mi (1–15 km) inside the Russian rear ([Armada International, Feb 2026](https://www.armadainternational.com/2026/02/the-curran-papers-no-5-jamming-uninhabited-aerial-vehicle-video-signals-electronic-warfare/)). Ukraine is building its own 930 mi (1,500 km) belt of detectors and jammers ([Euromaidan Press](https://euromaidanpress.com/2025/08/10/when-the-beeping-starts-someones-drone-is-about-to-die/)). Trench and vehicle jammers on both sides add more, close to the troops.
- **They cover our band.** "Most mobile jammers could suppress narrowband ISM frequencies, namely the 868MHz to 915 MHz, 2.4 GHz, and 5.8 GHz bands", and FPV control links (ExpressLRS) run on Semtech's LoRa chips, the same chips our darts use ([Armada](https://www.armadainternational.com/2026/02/the-curran-papers-no-5-jamming-uninhabited-aerial-vehicle-video-signals-electronic-warfare/)). Russia's Groza-07K covers 100 MHz–1.1 GHz, and "Tishina" is a prototype built to find and suppress LoRa ([Armada, Sep 2026](https://www.armadainternational.com/2026/09/russia-tishina-software-defined-jammer-electronic-warfare/)). 433 MHz is not in the mobile jammers' list.
- **LoRa networks are in use anyway.** Ukrainian units run Meshtastic LoRa meshes on 433 and 868 MHz ([Meshtastic forum](https://meshtastic.discourse.group/t/using-in-ukraine/5685)). A thread there argues low-power LoRa bursts are hard to locate; that is an opinion, not a test.
- **Not found:** any measurement of how far from a jammer a LoRa link near the ground still works, how often jammers are switched on, or any report of a LoRa sensor network being jammed in the field. Our [jamming model]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link models/line-layout.md %}#jamming) estimates the distance; T19 has to measure it.

## What infantry use to hear drones today

- **Radio detectors, not microphones.** Handheld detectors warn of nearby drones but don't tell friendly from enemy ([Stars and Stripes](https://www.stripes.com/theaters/europe/2024-10-28/ukraine-farming-vital-battlefield-land-15653560.html)), and are "rendered useless" against fibre-optic drones, which emit nothing ([Kyiv Independent](https://kyivindependent.com/as-russias-fiber-optic-drones-flood-the-battlefield-ukraine-is-racing-to-catch-up/)).
- **Acoustic detection of fibre-optic FPVs is being tried.** Claim: they are louder because they carry a cable spool, but most microphones struggle to pick drones out of background noise beyond about 330 ft ([Euromaidan Press, Feb 2025](https://euromaidanpress.com/2025/02/03/russias-fiber-optic-drones-dodge-jamming-but-ukraine-hunts-them-with-infrared-and-sound/)). That matches [VolAnti's measured 340 ft]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link research/open-source-builds.md %}#volanti).
- **The sky is full of friendly drones.** Friendly observation, bomber and resupply drones fly over the same tree lines, sometimes for nights at a time ([Texty](https://texty.org.ua/articles/117451/why-ukrainians-soldiers-havent-left-their-combat-positions-for-months/)). **Not found:** any reporting on whether this confuses acoustic detectors.
- **Debris everywhere.** Fields and streets are covered in "spider webs" of spent optical fibre ([Kyiv Independent](https://kyivindependent.com/as-russias-fiber-optic-drones-flood-the-battlefield-ukraine-is-racing-to-catch-up/)), and mines are hidden under leaves ([Texty](https://texty.org.ua/articles/117451/why-ukrainians-soldiers-havent-left-their-combat-positions-for-months/)).
- **Not found:** any reporting on unattended ground sensors dropped by drone in Ukraine, on how often such equipment is lost, or on how many seconds of warning units say they need.

## What this means for our models

These are our inferences from the sources above, not findings in them.

| Condition | Consequence for the design | Where it lands |
|---|---|---|
| Infantry, approach routes and fibre-optic FPVs all follow tree lines | The forest case of the radio test is the one that matters, not the open-field case. Lines should cross tree lines and cover the gaps between them, since that is where approaching drones and walking troops go | [Tripwire]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link models/tripwire.md %}), [T9]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link research/open-questions.md %}#t9-can-a-forward-line-report-back) |
| Leaves on and off | Sound and radio both pass through bare winter tree lines better than summer ones. Test both seasons | T9, T11 |
| GNSS is jammed | The drop drone can't count on GPS, and nodes can't count on it to report where they landed. A node's position can come from the drop point in the drone's mission instead. Navigation without GPS is its own open question | [Delivery drone]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link research/delivery-drone.md %}), T14 |
| Jammers on both sides cover 868–915 MHz | A jammer blinds a dart only from a few hundred feet on our estimate, but miles in the worst case. Our own jammers sit beside our receivers, so links at the positions and on vehicles go by wire or fibre, not radio | [How it works]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link models/line-layout.md %}#jamming), [Guarding a road]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link models/route-guard.md %}#jamming-and-a-wire-along-the-road), T19 |
| Emitters are located and struck | Keep node bursts rare and short, and the receiver at the unit silent. Measure what a node looks like to a direction-finder before fielding | T8 |
| Fibre-optic FPVs emit nothing and radio detectors miss them | Confirms the reason this project exists: sound is one of the few cheap ways to hear them | Payoff scores |
| Friendly drones fill the same sky | A detector that alarms on every drone is useless. Friend-or-foe needs a plan: friendly flight schedules, known friendly sound signatures, or both | T15 |
| Detection range near 330 ft in real noise | Supports node spacing toward the low end of the 660–980 ft planning figure, or closer in wind | T11 |
| Cold winters | Hobby-grade batteries and boards need a winter derating; choose cells rated for −4 °F | T12 |
| Months-long holds, resupply by drone | Nodes must need no maintenance and be re-laid by drone, which is how the tripwire is designed | Tripwire |
