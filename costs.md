<details class="layer" id="short-answer" markdown="block">
<summary><span class="layer-title">Short answer</span><span class="layer-sub">The package, fibre, the price, solar, roads and the next lever, in six bullets</span></summary>

- **One unit's package costs us about $3,510 today,** about $2,900 in a small batch and about $2,060 in production. That is 22 darts, 4 tip darts with their fibre, a base box wired to the positions, and a delivery drone with its navigation and confirm kit, 6 packs and a 2-channel charger. Every dart carries a small solar panel and a 4-mic array that takes its own bearing.
- **Fibre made it smaller.** A lay takes about 22 darts, against about 35 on radio trunks: 16 planned on real ground, plus about 6 for bad landings, short hops and broken fibres. The drone is now about half the package.
- **The price is our cost plus our markup:** +200–300% on the darts, tip darts, base box and wire, which are ours, and +30% on the off-the-shelf drone kit. The package sells for about $6,340–7,840 in a small batch, about $2,110–2,610 per position.
- **Solar makes re-lays a winter job.** A dart's panel covers its daily use from March to October, so cells run flat only in midwinter. We plan on 4 winter re-lays a year, 2 if winters are kind: about $1,600–6,300 a year per unit at our cost, against about $10,600–20,600 at four-week cells without solar. A second cell in every dart might cut winter re-lays to 0–1 ([below](#keeping-it-up)). How much a panel collects in a Ukrainian winter (T12) is now the biggest number in the budget.
- **A road costs more than a position.** Guarding 4 mi of supply road takes a package of about $6,500–7,900 and about $20–30k a year with solar, $5,000–7,300 a mile ([below](#guarding-a-road)).
- **Landing reliability (T10) is the next lever:** at 95% upright instead of 85%, each lay uses about 2 fewer darts.

</details>

**Fibre is now the tip's biggest cost,** and its price is rising. Bare bend-insensitive single-mode fibre (G.657.A2), the kind FPV spools use, was quoted at $33–35 a km in March 2026, about $53–56 a mile. Ready-wound 50 km spools went from about $300 to about $2,500 over the year, about $80 a mile, as AI data centres took the same fibre ([DroneXL](https://dronexl.co/2026/05/11/ukraine-fiber-optic-spool-price-ai-data-center-demand/)). Retail 10 km FPV spools are listed at about €200 ([Elfcams](https://www.elfcams.com/us/product/29204)), about $37 a mile. We plan on $55–80 a mile. **The laser is cheap:** the 1310 nm laser in fibre-to-the-home modems comes already joined to a lead of the same fibre, and is listed at $5–9, buy-one minimum ([GoldSupplier](https://www.goldsupplier.com/provide/p100619675.html), one listing, unconfirmed).

The small batch costs about the same per dart as the prototype because it pays for assembly. Bulk pricing saves about what the labour adds. The phone or tablet that shows the alarm is the unit's own Android phone, so it is not in the package. The tip dart, the base box and the wire are a design, not yet built ([the fibres and the base box]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link models/line-layout.md %}#the-fibres-and-the-base-box)); their prices are estimates in every column.

Darts are costed at the middle of each range. **What the lay model added to the drone:** the flow-and-lidar sensor ($42), the confirm radio ($20), 4 more packs and a second charger ($340) and a pack bag ($20), about $420 in all. A unit that already flies an ArduPilot quad can take the package without the drone and its packs: darts, tip darts, base box and wire, harness, flow sensor and confirm radio, about $1,570 in a small batch.

**What solar and the fibre price changed:** solar adds about $3 a dart in a small batch, and the fibre's rise adds about $28 a tip dart. Together they put about $180 on the package, and they cut the yearly bill by two-thirds ([below](#keeping-it-up)).

**What the mic array changed:** four mics, an audio chip and a compass in place of one mic add about $5.50 a dart in a small batch and about $2 in production, about $145 on a small-batch package. At prototype prices it is about $18 a dart, because the audio board is ordered a few at a time. The three extra mics wake only when the first hears a drone, so the daily power budget is unchanged (est.).

**What fibre changed:** the last plan, radio trunks, carried 40 darts and 3 receivers for about $2,620 in a small batch, and still needed about $60–100 of forward roots on fibre to survive the unit's own jammers. Fibre to the front row drops 12 relay darts and the receivers, adds 4 tip darts, a base box and a wire, and needs nothing more for our own jammers.

<details class="layer" id="keeping-it-up" markdown="block">
<summary><span class="layer-title">Keeping it up</span><span class="layer-sub">Re-lays, solar through a Donetsk winter, repairs, and what T10 and T12 decide</span></summary>

Darts aren't recovered. Every net is re-laid when its cells run down, and darts lost to shelling are replaced. **A re-lay is about 22 darts and 7 sorties:** about 20 darts and 2.6 tip darts on average. The drone comes home, except about 0.15 times a lay, at the model's guess of 2% lost per sortie. The base box and the wire stay.

**Solar sets how often.** A dart listens all the time to relay its neighbours, about 105 mAh a day. A 2.4 in panel lying flat on its pod collects about that much in November and more from March to October, but only about half of it in December and two-thirds in January (PVGIS sunlight records for Donetsk oblast, 2015–2020, est.). Over November to January a dart falls about 3,200 mAh short, more than its cell holds, so the net still goes flat in midwinter. Snow, shade in tree belts and frost, when the charger must not charge, make it worse.

| Power | Re-lays a year | Our cost at prototype (~$1,590 a re-lay) | Our cost in production (~$810 a re-lay, est.) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Solar, 4 winter re-lays (the plan)** | 4 | **~$6,350** | **~$3,260** |
| Solar, 2 winter re-lays | 2 | ~$3,170 | ~$1,630 |
| Solar and a second cell in every dart (option) | 0–1 | ~$0–1,710 | ~$0–880 |
| No solar, 4-week cells | 13 | ~$20,600 | ~$10,600 |
| No solar, 2-week cells | 26 | ~$41,300 | ~$21,200 |

A re-lay's cost is about 22 darts, plus 0.15 of a drone (about $140 at prototype prices, $80 in production), plus about $2 of pack wear. **Repairs between re-lays** cost one dart each, about $35–40, or one tip dart, about $128, plus about $0.30 of pack wear a sortie; up to 4 darts go on one sortie ([When it breaks]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link models/line-layout.md %}#when-it-breaks)). With solar, losses to shelling are a bigger share of the bill than before.

**A second cell could end winter re-lays (an option, not in the package).** Over November to January a dart falls about 3,200 mAh short, and one 18650 holds about 2,500. A second cell and holder, about $3–5.50 a dart and 1.6 oz, doubles that to about 5,000 mAh, or about 3,500–4,000 mAh if the cells keep 70–80% of their capacity in the cold (est.). That just covers the shortfall on paper, so the net might need 0–1 winter re-lays, and the yearly bill becomes mostly darts lost to shelling, which this table doesn't price for a position. It adds about $80–145 to a package. Not yet checked: the heavier dart (4.5 oz to about 6.1 oz) in the lay model, its fit in the 3D model, and whether the margin holds through a real winter.

None of this is measured. T12 now has two halves: how long a cell lasts, and how much a panel collects through a winter, with the cold-charging cutoff. It is the biggest number in the whole budget. **T10 is next:** each dart that lands upright instead of tilted saves one re-drop, and at 95% upright a lay uses about 20 darts instead of 22, about $300 a year at small-batch prices and 4 re-lays.

</details>

- **Solar cut the bill by more than half.** Re-lays fall from 13 a year to 2–4, and posts stop needing cell swaps: a post's tilted 1 W panel collects about twice its daily use even in December, and its 4 cells hold about 100 days in reserve (est.).
- **Repairs are now half the bill,** about $9,900–13,100 a year: darts and posts lost to shelling, and cut fibres. Battery life no longer dominates; losses do, and those guesses (0.5% of darts and 1% of posts a day) are now the numbers to check.
- **The fence is 81–87% of the bill.** Lost posts are the rest.
- **Fibre made it cheaper.** Each fibre replaces about 5 relay darts. Its price rose in 2026, but at $55–80 a mile it is still the cheaper trunk.
- **Cut fibres are a running cost:** at a guessed 1% of days each, about 18–26 new tip darts a year, about $2,600–3,800.
- **The drone is the hidden cost again:** at 2% lost per sortie, a re-lay loses about $270–500 of drone.
- **Posts only,** with no fence: about $1,530 for 25 posts and 14 receivers, and about $3,800 a year, all of it posts lost (about $940 a mile). It hears drones land by the road but gives only 4–9 s of warning of a strike.
- **Zvook's NW0** is pitched for roads at 1,300–2,000 ft spacing, which is about 11–17 sensors on 4 mi, about $9–16k before power, with no fence ([competitors]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link research/competitors.md %})).

**What the market charges, for comparison** (all from [the competitor analysis]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link research/competitors.md %})):
