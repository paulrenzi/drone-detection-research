# Changelog

| Date | Change |
|---|---|
| 2026-10-15 | Repositioned around [two markets]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link research/competitors.md %}#two-markets-only-one-of-them-ours). Cheap acoustic networks (Sky Fortress, Zvook stations, FENEK) are proven against Shaheds over the rear, not against FPVs at the front. The US Army's March 2026 review claims FPV detection, but its sources are about Shaheds. Changes: <ul><li>Our competition at a position is now named: a soldier's own ears (unmeasured), the radio detector as partner ("the second box"), and Zvook NW0.</li><li>NW0 is now marked maker's claims only: no price, test or deployment.</li><li>New tagline on the overview and competitors page.</li><li>Added the tripwire's [running cost]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link models/tripwire.md %}#the-running-cost-lines-are-ammunition) (estimated $5–16k a year in re-lays), which the kit price left out.</li></ul> |
| 2026-10-15 | [Front-line conditions]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link research/field-conditions.md %}#where-infantry-actually-sit-tree-lines): no published figure gives how far positions sit from a tree-line edge; added the sourced context (belt widths, burrows, watched belts, real forest) and labelled the distance estimates as inference. T18 now also asks what terrain a position is in and how far it is from the tree edge. The tripwire notes the wire to the edge works in every terrain; the open risk is the listener-to-edge radio leg (T9 run E). |
| 2026-10-15 | Added [What a minute buys]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link research/problem-check.md %}#what-a-minute-buys): what troops can actually do with warning against fibre-optic FPVs. Mostly avoidance (cover, holding movement), plus readying shotgun teams and cueing the AI turrets now in front-line units; nets and cable traps don't benefit. T18 now asks which moments matter. Corrected the tripwire's claim that a minute lets a unit launch an interceptor. |
| 2026-10-15 | Put the front-line evidence where readers see it first: a **What the front tells us** summary on the [overview]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link index.md %}), a **Why it matters** section on the [tripwire]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link models/tripwire.md %}), and a line in the [competitor positioning]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link research/competitors.md %}). The tripwire's payoff of 5 is now backed by sources; feasibility stays at 3 until T9, T17 and T18 report. |
| 2026-10-15 | Added [Is the problem worth solving?]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link research/problem-check.md %}): drones cause about 80% of casualties, troops survive mainly by staying underground, and radio detectors miss fibre-optic FPVs. Anduril's WISP is not in use at the front. No source says how much warning troops need. Zvook's NW0 acoustic FPV sensor (Jan 2026) added to competitors. New test T18: ask front-line users before building more. |
| 2026-10-15 | Tripwire: treated the radio problem as engineering. New section [Getting alerts out of the trees]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link models/tripwire.md %}#getting-alerts-out-of-the-trees): windbreak belts are 3–15 m wide and cost a few dB each on paper, so a listen-only receiver placed by hand at the edge of the unit's tree line, raised and wired back to the dugout, may carry what a receiver under the trees can't, with nothing at the unit transmitting. Added [Questions we've weighed]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link models/tripwire.md %}#questions-weve-weighed) (hands or a drone, whether it's worth it, retrieving listeners, a central anchor, predicting radio range), a [simulation method and nearest research]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link research/open-questions.md %}#predicting-it-before-the-field-test) for T9, a windbreak run to T9 and test T17. |
| 2026-10-15 | Considered a reader's suggestion: anchor nodes to one central node instead of GPS on every node. Added a [short note]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link models/tripwire.md %}#solving-the-field-limits-at-no-extra-cost) to the tripwire (it already works this way and doesn't need ranging), a GPS-jamming risk to [M1]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link models/m01-ground-array-mesh.md %}) and [M3]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link models/m03-single-mic-tdoa.md %}), and test [T16]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link research/open-questions.md %}#t16-positions-and-clocks-without-gps) with published SX1280 ranging results. T15 now lists a time beacon as a sync option. |
| 2026-10-15 | Reorganised the [tripwire]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link models/tripwire.md %}) page into layers: a one-screen summary, the front-line problems and fixes in plain terms, then fold-out sections for the full detail. Listeners are now costed without GPS throughout. |
| 2026-10-15 | Tripwire: [solved the field limits at no extra cost]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link models/tripwire.md %}#solving-the-field-limits-at-no-extra-cost). Nodes drop GPS (about $17–50 each instead of $25–65), friend and foe are sorted by which line trips first plus the unit's flight windows, and alerts carry sound, battery and temperature summaries, so each line becomes a record of drone traffic, jamming, radio range and winter battery life. Proposed, not tested; T12 and T15 updated. |
| 2026-10-15 | Added two research notes: [Front-line conditions in Ukraine]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link research/field-conditions.md %}) (tree lines, seasons, the kill zone, jamming, what infantry use to hear drones) and [Delivery drone]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link research/delivery-drone.md %}) (airframes, ArduPilot missions with drop steps, open-source software, flying under GPS jamming). Fed both into the tripwire and M7, and added tests T14 (laying a line without GPS) and T15 (ignoring friendly drones). |
| 2026-10-15 | Reviewed published ground-level LoRa range measurements for the tripwire's deciding question (T9): likely to work in open ground, unproven in forest. Added the evidence and a field test with pass/fail criteria to Open questions, and updated the tripwire's limits. Tripwire part photos now also show on the tripwire page. |
| 2026-10-15 | Added a Components and MVP section to the drone-laid tripwire: a per-node parts table and a five-step MVP path, linked to the weekend test kit. |
| 2026-10-15 | Added a weekend test kit (parts with photos, an ~$80–90 MVP and a weekend plan) to Open-source projects. Corrected the claim that VolAnti's four-mic board could give bearings. |
| 2026-10-15 | Added a landscape summary to the overview: what exists, what works, what failed and why, and the routes still open. |
| 2026-10-15 | Added a **Payoff** score next to feasibility on the matrix and every model page; added the tripwire to the matrix. Added a research note on open-source projects to build on (Batear, VolAnti, AcousticPrint) and linked it from range, training data and the tripwire. |
| 2026-10-15 | Added the drone-laid tripwire use case: cheap throwaway listeners dropped 2–4 km forward by a 3D-printed delivery drone, compared honestly against phones and other sensors. Added tests T9–T13. |
